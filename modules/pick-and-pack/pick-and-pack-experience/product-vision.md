The primary objective is to establish Pick and Pack as the purpose-built fulfillment operations tool for grocery merchants scaling omnichannel operations — measured by average time to pick.*

## Problem / Opportunity

### 1. Narrative framing

Retailers struggle to profitably meet shoppers’ heightened post-pandemic expectations for fast, reliable, convenient, and low-cost fulfillment. Average parcel delivery speed in the US and Europe has accelerated by about 40 percent, going from 6.6 days in the first quarter of 2020 to 4.2 days in the second quarter of 2023. [2] When trying to leverage physical stores for ship-from-store or pickup, they face high costs, integration complexity, and operational inefficiencies. This results in reliance on expensive third-party solutions (Janis, Instaleap, Síntese) or on-demand delivery apps (iFood, Rappi, DoorDash, Glovo) that charge commissions up to 20%. ### 2. Why Now

The urgency is driven by:
a. Shoppers expecting faster, more flexible fulfillment options — 90% consider 2–3 day shipping a baseline, ~30% expect same-day, and 35% actively use BOPIS — pushing merchants to leverage every physical location, from stores to darkstores. [3]
b. Rising fuel, labor, and last-mile costs eroding margins, [2] with shoppers unwilling to absorb these (a last-mile delivery costs the organization an average of $10.1, but the customer only pays an average $8.08 [10]); grocers are particularly affected, since their ability to use fees or higher prices to improve gross margins is limited [18].
c. Stores facing inventory accuracy gaps (70–90% vs. 99.5% in warehouses) and longer picking cycles (4–8 hours),  [4] since they are not designed for fulfillment.
d. Competitors (Janis, Instaleap, Síntese) offering costly alternatives [15], leaving a gap for an integrated VTEX-native product with  lower onboarding and recurring costs.
e. VTEX transitioning to a suite of solutions with the vision of becoming the backbone for connected commerce [19], with VTEX Pick and Pack representing GMV expansion by offering the connection between offline and online for omnichannel operations.
This can lead to customer churn, missed upsell opportunities, and difficulties to stay competitive in omnichannel fulfillment.

### 3. Opportunity size

**What should we focus on?** The primary audience for VTEX Pick and Pack is large and medium-sized retailers with physical stores who want to leverage the omnichannel experience, where rising cost pressures, tight margins and offline with online inventory management are critical points. Any merchant fulfilling from physical store can be benefitted by VTEX Pick and Pack, due to the nature of the issues listed above; however, our Ideal Customer Profile includes the following segments, in order of relevance: 1. groceries and 2. pharma, due to the need for fast deliveries, order changes and managing high assortment levels; and 3. fashion, which deals with high store footprint and the pressure to operate more efficiently with the current labour capacity. 

Smaller operations (<5 orders/store/day, less than 5 stores per merchant) are not included since the need for a fulfillment software is reduced due to the low complexity and costs of order handling. We want to prioritize opportunities from both store front (which may synergize with Sales App) and backrooms, plus darkstores or mid-sized warehouses. Larger warehouses or distribution centers (>5k orders/day/location) are not our target audience, since the complex needs of operating in this scale with multiple hardwares and highly specific software business rules are already addressed in the market with established WMS or ERP systems.

We can split the size of the opportunity into some key groups (TAM breakdown can be found in the Appendix tab): 
i. current VTEX customer base in the segments listed above - The TAM (Total Addressable Market) of upselling is USD 28 million of ACV; 
ii. new VTEX logos with Omnichannel strategy or a new revenue stream with a standalone DOM offer bundled with Pick and Pack. A large share of the market still relies on outdated, homegrown OMS solutions or heavily customized ERP connectors that no longer scale, and they fail to provide near real-time, omnichannel inventory visibility at the critical moments that influence cart abandonment. [14] For new Omnichannel logos or a standalone DOM (groceries, pharma and fashion), the TAM is USD 133 million of ACV.

Given that VTEX Pick and Pack still has some gaps to address, based on the High-Level Phasing plan we should focus first on Groceries with the development of Phase 2. Fashion should come next with Phase 3, while Phase 4 will strengthen our Pharma value proposition. Detailed ACV by region can be found in Add-ons Strategy Review file.

### 4. Customer Workarounds

Today, merchants rely on expensive third-party tools [15] or high-commission marketplaces to prepare orders from physical stores (which are not designed for fulfillment at large scale), but these solutions erode margins and require extra implementation effort to integrate with VTEX platform.

Store managers also heavily rely on Excel or Sheets dashboards to track fulfillment performance. This fails because it is always lagging (not real-time) and requires manual maintenance.

## Vision Concepts

These are value statements that will guide us on the process of designing VTEX Pick and Pack evolutions and the reasons why they are relevant to VTEX Pick and Pack.

- **Mobility first and easy-to-use.** Our primary persona can have a variety of age ranges and usually work outside of a desk. We want our solution to be easily accessible by all operators in this environment.
- **Modular, API-parity and extensible.** Applying a software in large scale fulfillment operations requires a degree of freedom to set up the solution and integrations (DOM, Sales App). We want to support the core picking and packing rules used by enterprise merchants. Our solution will expose extensibility points at key decision steps — such as order prioritization, picker assignment, and item substitution — allowing merchants and AI agents to plug in custom logic without replacing the core fulfillment flow.
- **Interoperable with common in-store hardwares and software.** Mid to large size retailers operate with some level of hardware and software investment (POS systems in Brazil, SalesApp barcode readers, TMS, printers). We want merchants to be able to build integrations between our solution and devices/software, by exposing APIs and integration protocols that allow third-party integrators and partners to connect.
- **Native VTEX solution.** Merchants have to deal with high acquisition costs from 3p vendors/long time to market with fulfillment competitors. We want our solution to be able to be activated seamlessly if they already have the VTEX platform on and use the most of existing modules and features available (Catalog, OMS, Logistics).
- **AI-ready.** Shoppers and merchants can benefit from the power of AI during fulfillment, enabling faster order handling, greater autonomy in shopper interactions, and proactive store monitoring and management. Our solution will be designed to seamlessly integrate with AI agents.
- **Global.** Our addressable market incorporates multiple continents. We want our solution to be fully localized, follow local regulatory and data privacy requirements, and be ready to support regional ecosystem integrations through extensible APIs and partner-led implementations.
- **Real-time fulfillment data aggregator.** Shoppers, store managers, customer support agents currently lack visibility about the fulfillment operation, and also relies on multiple spread sources of operational data. We want merchants to be able to leverage AI with our centralized data to redesign expedition strategies and enhance other VTEX modules (such as Logistics, OMS, Order Allocation) with real-time information.
- **Resilient in unstable connectivity environments.** Store operators in large retail locations may face unstable network signals. We want our solution to handle temporary connectivity drops in critical fulfillment steps so it doesn't increase picking or packing times.
- **High availability and reliability.** VTEX Pick and Pack is a critical part of merchants' fulfillment operations — downtime directly impacts order handling and delivery promises. Our solution must maintain high uptime, minimizing disruptions to store operations.
- **Agentic Fulfillment Experience** — In VTEX Pick and Pack, "agentic" means the system
acts proactively to resolve exceptions and optimize operations without requiring
manual intervention. Operationally this means:
(1) AI surfaces exceptions (stockouts, picker delays, SLA risks) and suggests
    resolution actions to store managers;
(2) AI autonomously routes orders to optimal pickers based on capacity and proximity;
(3) AI suggests item substitutions in a one-tap interface when items are unavailable;
(4) Shopper-facing conversational interfaces (via VTEX CX) enable real-time order
    modifications during fulfillment.

## Vision Statement

**3-Year Vision:** In 3 years, any store associate at a grocery, pharma, or fashion retailer will fulfill orders in the minimum physically possible time — with AI routing picks optimally, suggesting substitutions without friction, and giving operations teams real-time performance visibility — all natively within VTEX, with no dependency on expensive third-party tools.

**1-Year Vision (H2 2025 – H1 2026):** Phase 1 (real-time OMS sync) is live across active merchants. Phase 2 scan-first alpha is deployed with at least 2 Tier 1 merchants operational. Average picking time per order is measurable for the first time via a self-service dashboard — giving the team and merchants a baseline to improve against. Cost per order is on a verified trajectory toward the $0.08 target.

We want to be the easiest to activate, configure, and extend — a self-service-first, modular solution that works for both express grocery operations and high-footprint fashion stores, natively integrated with VTEX OMS, and architected to support AI-driven fulfillment and regional ecosystem integrations. We expect to help merchants *expedite orders faster and cheaper*, reducing reliance on costly 3p solutions and increasing long-term retention in VTEX.


### Key Capabilities

To help our target merchants to deliver greater convenience to shoppers while controlling cost to serve in an omnichannel reality, we believe VTEX Pick and Pack can provide the following key capabilities:

- **Scan-first Experience.** Order-centric user experience using scanning from multiple hardwares (regular or phones with built-in bar code readers) as a primary action. Allows faster fulfillment in stores. Reduces the cognitive load when dealing with SKU complexity, to improve picking accuracy. Suited for fast or express deliveries or pickups (SLA of 1 hour or less). Target audience: groceries and pharma.
- **Intelligent Order Transfer.** Leverage the power of VTEX Order Allocation in the scenario of store stockouts during fulfillment, by seeing insights on Pick and Pack to better decide how to transfer an order. Stores will be able to see the costs of order transfer to fulfill according to the delivery promise, create campaigns based on shipping and service fees accordingly. Target audience: pharma and fashion.
- **Real-Time Order Modifications.** Sync order changes from OMS to Pick and Pack in real time. Information from Pick and Pack can also be supplied to VTEX OMS, which can be connected with shoppers to notify about order updates and change their order in real time. Drives customer satisfaction with a convenience model. Target audience: groceries and fashion.
- **Exchanges powered by AI.** Enables shoppers to modify an order (replacements, adding new items, removals) while it's being handled, using conversational interfaces powered by AI. Target audience: groceries.
- **Picking Modes and Rules.** Multiple modes to increase stores productivity in the scenario of high footprint stores/high order volume/low workforce: multipicking, batch, wave and zone picking. Picker assignment and routing enhanced by AI. Capacity increase can be translated into costs reduction for merchants and possible new shipping fees strategies for shoppers, when facing strong competition. Target audience: groceries and fashion.
- **Last Mile Carrier Assignment and Visibility.** Automatically request shipping carriers to orders already packed using VTEX Pick and Pack items and package data (quantity, weight, size) and access real-time order tracking with shipper details. Reduce manual errors, allows carriers selected to get package dimensions and provides visibility to deliver on time in full. Target audience: groceries and pharma.
- **Sales App Integration.** Allows fulfillment from stores in which the salesperson is also responsible for order and returns processing. Sales App can consume Pick and Pack's APIs and reusable UI components — such as the barcode reader — to build their own fulfillment frontend on top of Pick and Pack's backend rules and VTEX Platform integrations. Target audience: pharma and fashion.
- **Control Tower and Insights.** A view with live-data feed for store managers to quickly check status, personnel performance and exceptions to be managed together with AI agents. Visibility to ensure orders are delivered on time, in full, in any delivery SLA. Insights from historical data supported with a conversational experience. Target audience: any segment.
- **Real-Time Fulfillment Data for VTEX Platform.** Pick and Pack operational data — such as picker availability, throughput per store, and scanned item information — is shared in real time with other VTEX modules to improve platform-wide decisions. Order Allocation can use live picker capacity to route new orders away from fully booked stores. Catalog can leverage scanning data to detect inconsistencies such as incorrect EANs or mismatched product images. Target audience: any segment.

### Non-Goals

- We will not focus on large warehouse operations (>5k orders/day/warehouse), nor small businesses ones (< 5 orders/day/store), since our solution will not be a WMS
- We will not support a fully offline picking and packing flow, since our premise is that fulfillment centers are able to provide internet connectivity. However, our solution will be designed to handle temporary connectivity drops in critical fulfillment steps without disrupting operations.
- We will not develop on VTEX Pick and Pack complex algorithms that require another layer of technical specialization (examples: operational research for planning or numerical optimization for last mile routing)
- We will not develop in-house POS integrations for Brazilian real-time invoicing, but support instead the work done by third-party integrators
- We will process orders from any sales channel, but they must pass through VTEX OMS (we will not connect directly with other OMS) and the integration should be defined before Pick and Pack activation

## High Level Phasing

Our recommendation is to keep Phases 1 and 2 as fixed priorities — they are already being developed (1) and designed (2). Phase 2 will bring grocery expansion and 3 is a direct request from Carrefour. Phases 4 and 5 are team-owned with no external dependencies and unlock retention ACV.
Phase 6 has a dependency on the Order Allocation and Order Management teams and is sequenced after the team-owned phases are delivered; however, it is a direct request from Marisa. Phases 7 and 8 are sequenced last among the core phases because its ACV unlock is indirect, but it enables platform stickiness and cross-module intelligence rather than a direct sales motion. 

- **Phase 1 - Real-Time Order Sync Between OMS and Pick and Pack.** Merchants looking to increase customer satisfaction and retention will be able to sync order changes from OMS to Pick and Pack in real time. This means that they avoid packing errors and reduce return rates during fulfillment.
- **Phase 2 - Scan-First Picking Experience for Faster Fulfillment.** Merchants looking to reduce average picking time will be able to adopt a scan-first picking process with a redesigned, order-oriented experience and automatic carrier assignment. This means that they lower fulfillment costs, and increase operational efficiency.
- **Phase 3 - AI-Powered Exchanges During Order Handling.** Merchants that offer order adjustments while they are prepared will be able to let shoppers modify through AI-driven conversational interfaces. This means they can reduce call center demand, increase shopper satisfaction, and boost operational flexibility.
- **Phase 4 - Control Tower for Real-Time Store Operations.** Merchants will be able to access a live-data Control Tower view to monitor order status, staff performance, and fulfillment exceptions. This means they can ensure SLA compliance, proactively resolve issues, and improve operational efficiency.
- **Phase 5 - Picking Modes and Rules for Fulfillment Cost Optimization.** Merchants looking to reduce operational costs will be able to configure picking modes such as multipicking, batch, wave, and zone picking, with AI-enhanced picker assignment and route suggestions for stores with high footprint. This means they can increase productivity and unlock new shipping fee strategies to stay competitive.
- **Phase 6 - Intelligent Order Transfer for Store Stockouts.** Merchants dealing with store stockouts will be able to leverage VTEX Order Allocation while accessing Pick and Pack to fulfill an order. This means they can reduce delivery promise breaches and optimize transfer costs.
- **Phase 7 - Sales App Integration for Store Fulfillment.** Merchants will be able to fulfill those and process returns directly through store salespeople, leveraging existing VTEX Platform integrations. This means they can lower operational costs, streamline in-store workflows, and improve service speed.
- **Phase 8 - Real-Time Fulfillment Data for VTEX Platform.** Merchants and other VTEX platform modules will be able to consume live Pick and Pack operational data — picker availability, store throughput, and item-level scan information — via platform APIs. This means Order Allocation can route incoming orders away from fully booked stores in real time, and Catalog can leverage scanning data to detect inventory inconsistencies such as mismatched EANs or incorrect product images.

## Hotly Debated Topics

**1. Sales team adoption of Pick and Pack as a commercial priority.**
The sales team has shown resistance to positioning Pick and Pack against established competitors (Instaleap, Janis) given the product's current maturity level. Phase 2 (scan-first redesign) is the prerequisite for a confident commercial motion. The open question is: what is the sales readiness trigger? 
Current team position: Phase 2 alpha live with at least one Tier 1 merchant (Atacadão or Carrefour) is the minimum viable commercial proof point.

**2. Onboarding complexity as a structural constraint.** Mid-tier merchants (e.g., Supermercados Líder, onboarding since 2025) have remained in extended cycles. This is a product gap (activation is hard or confusing).
Phase 1 and 2 simplify the core activation path for standard merchants. Merchants requiring complex POS or TMS integrations must be qualified earlier in the sales cycle and routed to PS support — not absorbed into standard onboarding.

**3. What is the right scope for AI substitution suggestions?** Substitution rules vary significantly by merchant (grocery allows brand substitution; pharma does not allow generic substitution; fashion requires size/color confirmation from the customer). The AI model must be designed with configurable substitution policies before it can be applied across merchant types.

**3. Agentic Workflow entry point dependency.** Injecting Pick and Pack status events into the OMS order timeline depends on the Agentic Workflow entry point API, which is in RFC stage. 

**4. Sales team commercial readiness for Pick and Pack.** The sales team has hesitated to position Pick and Pack against established competitors (Instaleap, Janis) given the product's current maturity. Phase 2 (scan-first redesign) is the prerequisite for a confident commercial motion. Current team position: Phase 2 alpha live with one Tier 1 merchant operational is the minimum viable commercial proof point.

**5. Onboarding complexity as a qualification and product gap.** Mid-tier merchants in extended onboarding cycles are consuming team capacity without contributing to ARR. The debate is whether this is a product gap (activation is too complex) or a qualification gap (merchants with complex POS or TMS dependencies are being onboarded without PS routing). Current position: both — Phase 1 and 2 simplify the standard activation path; merchants with non-standard integrations must be qualified earlier and routed to PS support.

## FAQs

1. **What is the vision behind integrating the Sales App with Pick and Pack?**

   The vision is to unify the sales and fulfillment experience, focusing on store staff who handle both in-person sales and order preparation from specific segments (fashion with low store footprint, for example), often in storefront locations. This way, they will be able to locate inventory, pick and pack items, modify orders when necessary, and request delivery carriers — all within the same environment and backed by real-time synchronization with VTEX OMS provided by Pick and Pack, which should reduce order handling time and improve visibility into the order lifecycle. The integration model is not a merged or embedded app — Sales App will consume Pick and Pack's APIs and reusable UI components to build their own fulfillment frontend, focused on the salesperson's needs, while leveraging Pick and Pack's backend business rules and VTEX Platform integrations.

2. **What is our vision regarding product maturity and gaps identified through customer feedback?**

   Our solution is still in the early growth phase of its product lifecycle — beyond an MVP, but not yet at full product–market fit. The experience with our most recent churns showed a maturity gap between customer expectations and product readiness, resulting in almost one semester of development effort to cover the gaps. Our current focus is to strengthen the product's foundation, working on non-functional improvements such as observability and performance. We also plan to maintain a voice-of-customer roadmap based on vision-aligned feedback — for example, the Rona requirement to synchronize Pick and Pack with OMS in real time.

3. **Should our product be API-first?**

   Not necessarily. Our goal is to ensure that Pick and Pack can integrate within complex operations typical of our ICP (POS, printers, TMS, etc.). While we don't need to be fully API-first, maintaining a level of API parity is essential to provide flexibility, accelerate onboarding, and enable connections with other third-party systems.

4. **What is not Pick and Pack?**

   Pick and Pack is not a desktop solution — it is designed for mobile and in-store operations, not to compete with ERP, WMS or back-office picking tools like Síntese. It is not a point of sale system, as it does not generate fiscal invoices. It is not a standalone software; it operates fully within the VTEX OMS ecosystem, leveraging its data, workflows and integrations to execute fulfillment tasks efficiently.

5. **Does the implementation require an agency or PS support?**

   It depends. For merchants with simpler operations — typically up to 5–10 orders per store per day, fewer than 5 stores, and no specific architectural dependencies — the setup can be completed using our documentation available in VTEX Help, with the assistance of our Product Support team to help with any question. For larger or more complex operations, such as grocery retailers integrating Pick and Pack with their POS to issue fiscal invoices, or TMS and Carrier Hubs, we recommend searching for an implementation agency or adding Professional Services (PS) support. This additional layer of technical assistance ensures scalability and compliance.

6. **What is the biggest barrier to selling Pick and Pack?**

   The main barrier lies in the nature of the area this solution is aimed for: store-floor operations. It impacts supply chain processes, requiring merchants to train operators, adapt workflows or integrate with systems such as POS or TMS. These operational changes demand time, coordination, and buy-in from multiple teams, which can slow adoption. Compared to external solutions, Pick and Pack offers a faster activation within the VTEX ecosystem thanks to being a native addon. This advantage is most effective when targeting merchants already looking to adopt the full VTEX platform or those dissatisfied with their current fulfillment tools and committed to change.

7. **How will we measure the success of this product?**

   We expect to reduce delivery times by reducing the handling time of orders. This is our north star metric (picking/packing time). Additionally, we want to keep track of other operational efficiency metrics such as: store productivity (orders per hour per picker), picking accuracy (orders picked correctly), order modification or cancellation rate (post-purchase).

8. **What is the go-to-market strategy for Pick and Pack?**

   Please check [Add-ons Strategy Review](https://docs.google.com/document/d/17NPXE_gewad9vKozNuwIwg4W2Wo--uz5Mk1y_QTzodA/edit?tab=t.0) for more details.

9. **For which regions can we offer Pick and Pack today?**

   Americas for now. Pick and Pack currently supports English, French, Spanish and Portuguese. For Europe, development is necessary to comply with GDPR. Localization work is also required for other languages.

10. **What do we support for Last Mile today?**

    Only the addon, which covers back office last mile operations such as: integration with TMS and carrier hubs for carrier assignment, delivery tracking and confirmation. The mobile app was deprecated in 2024.

11. **What are our competitors, their strengths and weaknesses compared to VTEX Pick and Pack?**

    Please check [Add-ons Strategy Review](https://docs.google.com/document/d/17NPXE_gewad9vKozNuwIwg4W2Wo--uz5Mk1y_QTzodA/edit?tab=t.0) for more information (4. Pick and Pack Review > 6. VTEX Pick and Pack vs Competitors Analysis).

12. **Is there any difference between B2C and B2B strategies for Pick and Pack?**

    Not in terms of product evolution. We are designing our features to be the same for any type of merchant, looking for being as scalable as possible. We know there is a possibility of a large/enterprise company requiring some specific system integration (like Rona with shipping labels), but this should be an exception and not the rule. Pricing is also the same.

13. **Does Pick and Pack integrate with iFood/Uber Eats/Rappi?**

    If an order from these on-demand delivery apps integrates with OMS, Pick and Pack will be able to process it too. Currently, VTEX doesn't support a native integration with these apps. iFood, in particular, doesn't allow external integrations to enable the option for the customer to select which item should be picked in case another doesn't have stock.

## Top Risks

**1. Tier 1 merchant go-live before Phase 2 readiness.**
The current picking flow is known to be slow and fragmented. If a Tier 1 merchant goes fully operational before Phase 2 is ready, it risks repeating the churn pattern that Phase 2 was designed to fix. Tier 1 go-live timing must be sequenced against Phase 2 readiness — this is the single most important delivery dependency to track.

**2. Onboarding complexity extending activation cycles beyond viable cost.**
Extended onboarding cycles on non-Tier-1 delays generating ARR. Merchants with complex POS or TMS integration dependencies must be qualified in the sales cycle before onboarding begins. Setting up VTEX Pick and Pack will be easier after Phase 2, but using AI to facilitate this process should be assesed on the roadmap.

# TAM Breakdown

**Target Audience of upselling on current customer base**

Our TAM is USD 28 million of ACV, with 3,200 potential clients worldwide among VTEX considering the following segments: Apparel Accessories, Sport Fitness, Foods Beverages, Beauty Health, Cars and Autoparts, Electronics, Grocery, Home Appliances and Department Stores. If we look for tier 1 and 2 clients from this pipeline, we have an immediate reach valued at USD 23 million, with ~2,700 clients. For tier 2 and 3 segments with in-store fulfillment (White Label Stores, Sales App, Instore, Indeva), there are 398 clients with a potential market of USD 3.4 million.

**Target Audience of new logos for Omnichannel and standalone DOM**

**Groceries.** Global online grocery sales are expected to reach $725.6 billion in 2025 (US being ~45% of the global share). An average online supermarket transaction in the US totals $112. Globally, we can use a lower value of $60. If we estimate the number of online orders using this data and an estimated price per order of $0.01*, our TAM is USD 66 million of ACV globally for groceries only.

**Pharma.** The global online pharma market is estimated at $113.66 billion in 2025. Prescriptions and meds often have smaller baskets. If we assume ~$50 per order on average and an estimated price per order of $0.01*, our TAM is USD 22 million of ACV globally for pharma only.

**Fashion.** 48.0% of fashion retail sales worldwide are e-commerce transactions, for a total global revenue of $883.1 billion. The average number of apparel items ordered in a single transaction is 3.90, in which consumers spend an average of $50 per item. If we assume an estimated price per order of $0.01*, our TAM is USD 45 million of ACV globally for fashion only.

**Total value in these 3 verticals: USD 133 million of ACV.**
